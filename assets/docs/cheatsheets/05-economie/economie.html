Reading the economy: indicators, cycles, regimes

How a market digests macro data, and why the economic cycle drives major asset rotations.

GDP, expenditure approach GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)  — consumption, investment, government spending, net exports

Macro indicators

  • Leading (PMI, consumer confidence): anticipate the cycle.
  • Coincident (GDP, employment): describe the current state of the economy.
  • Lagging (inflation, unemployment): confirm a trend already in place.

Economic cycle

  • 4 phases: expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery.
  • Each phase favors different asset classes.
  • The cycle is never perfectly symmetric nor predictable in duration.

Economic regimes

  • Read on 2 axes: growth (high/low) and inflation (high/low).
  • 4 quadrants that drive asset allocation (equities, bonds, commodities, cash).
  • Regimes last from quarters to several years.

Monetary policy

  • Policy rates: central banks' main lever to steer inflation.
  • Quantitative easing/tightening: adjusts liquidity via the central bank's balance sheet.
  • Markets react as much to expectations as to the decisions themselves.

Who uses what?

Central banks Steer policy rates based on coincident and lagging indicators.
Macro strategists Position portfolios according to the cycle phase and the current regime.
Multi-asset managers Adjust equity/bond/commodity allocation based on the growth/inflation regime.
Economists Build scenarios and forecasts from leading indicators.

Key takeaways

  1. Leading indicators announce, coincident ones confirm, lagging ones validate — confusing them completely changes your reading of the cycle.
  2. The growth/inflation regime often matters more than the cycle alone when choosing between equities, bonds and commodities.
  3. Markets price monetary policy expectations well before the central bank's actual decision.

Real-world examples

2008 crisis

Abrupt shift from expansion to recession: credit collapse, flight to quality into government bonds.

Post-Covid recovery

V-shaped recovery fueled by massive monetary and fiscal policy, followed by an unexpected inflation spike.

2013 taper tantrum

The mere announcement of a slowdown in the Fed's quantitative easing was enough to trigger a sharp rise in long-term rates.

2022 hiking cycle

High and persistent inflation forced central banks into a rapid rate-hiking cycle, hurting equities and bonds simultaneously.

Quick glossary

PMI Purchasing Managers' Index, a key leading indicator of manufacturing/services activity.
Inverted yield curve Short-term rates above long-term rates, a historical warning signal for a coming recession.
Output gap Gap between actual and potential GDP of an economy.
Stagflation Rare combination of weak growth and high inflation.
Policy rate Rate set by the central bank, the reference for the entire cost of credit in the economy.
Flight to quality Investor shift toward the safest assets during periods of stress.
