Econometrics applied to finance

Time series, GARCH, non-linearities: the tools used to model financial data.

GARCH(1,1) — modeling volatility σt2 = ω + α·εt−12 + β·σt−12

Time series

  • Stationarity: stable mean and variance over time, a key condition for modeling.
  • ACF/PACF: tools to identify a series' autocorrelation structure.
  • A price series is rarely stationary; its returns often are much more so.

Volatility econometrics

  • ARCH/GARCH: model volatility clustering (calm and turbulent periods that group together).
  • σ depends on its own past and on recent shocks.
  • The backbone of most practical risk and options pricing models.

Non-linear models

  • Regime-switching models (Markov-switching): dynamics change depending on the market state.
  • Threshold models: behavior shifts once a critical threshold is crossed.
  • Capture dynamics that classic linear models miss.

Unit root tests

  • ADF test (Augmented Dickey-Fuller): checks whether a series is stationary.
  • A must before any regression on time series (otherwise spurious regression).
  • Complemented by cointegration tests for long-run relationships between series.

Who uses what?

Quant researcher Models price and volatility dynamics for pricing and risk purposes.
Risk modeler Uses GARCH to forecast short-horizon volatility within VaR models.
Econometrician Tests stationarity and cointegration before any macro modeling.
Data scientist Combines classic econometrics with machine learning for series forecasting.

Key takeaways

  1. Ignoring non-stationarity is mistake #1 in financial econometrics — it leads to spurious regressions with misleading results.
  2. Volatility clustering (calm/turbulent periods grouped together) is the whole reason GARCH models exist.
  3. Non-linear models don't replace linear ones: they complement them when the relationship between variables changes depending on the market context.

Real-world examples

Vol forecasting with GARCH

An options desk calibrates a GARCH(1,1) on returns to anticipate 1-day volatility and adjust its pricing.

Pairs trading

Two cointegrated stocks justify a pairs trading strategy: betting on the mean reversion of their price spread.

Macro VAR

A VAR model links GDP, inflation and the policy rate to simulate the effect of a monetary policy shock.

Regime change

A Markov-switching model automatically detects the shift from a low- to a high-volatility regime in equity markets.

Quick glossary

Stationarity Property of a series whose statistical characteristics don't change over time.
Autocorrelation Correlation of a series with its own past values.
Cointegration Stable long-run relationship between two series that are individually non-stationary.
Heteroscedasticity Residual variance that changes over time, typical of financial series.
Unit root Characteristic of a non-stationary series, tested via ADF.
Backtesting Validating a model on historical data before production use.
