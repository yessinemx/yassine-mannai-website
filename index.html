01 / About

Who I am

Tunis Lyon Paris

I'm the kind of person who needs to understand how something works all the way down — whether that's a stochastic volatility model, a football team's pressing scheme, or why a Grand Slam final turns on one point. That obsession with detail is what pulled me toward quantitative finance: I hold an MSc in Quantitative Finance (Master 272, Financial and Economic Engineering) from Université Paris Dauphine-PSL, and I currently work as a Quantitative Strategist with the Strategic Asset Allocation team of Generali France's Investment Division.

I'm equally comfortable in a spreadsheet and in a conversation — methodical and a little stubborn about getting things right, but also curious enough to keep pulling on unrelated threads (crypto, DJ sets, valuation multiples, Serie A transfer rumours) just to see where they lead. Professionally, that translates into a preference for methods that are rigorous — dynamic graphs, diffusion models, distributionally robust optimisation — but judged, first and foremost, by the real economic value they create.

I was born and raised in Tunis, Tunisia, where I did all my schooling before university and graduated with a Tunisian High School Degree (Baccalaureate) in Mathematics with highest honours — part of the “Covid Baccalaureate” cohort that sat its exams in July under 40°C heat. Moving from Tunis to Lyon and then to Paris on my own taught me to adapt fast and bet on myself, which is probably why I'm still comfortable weighing a handful of very different paths — a PhD, another Master's, a permanent role — instead of picking the safest-looking one.

Leaving a strict, conservative family home at eighteen — the only comfort zone I'd known for that long — to build a life on my own is probably the single biggest thing that shaped who I am today. It made me stubborn, independent and entirely self-reliant, and it turned me into someone who doesn't flinch at the size of a problem: whether it takes me an hour, a few days or a few weeks, I know I'll solve it, and I do it with a cold-blooded calm rather than stress. That's probably the trait people notice most about me — I stay chill and relaxed under pressure, and people know that if they hand me something, it gets done, no excuses.

I pay attention to the small things — the haircut, the outfit, the smile, the energy someone gives off — because I believe that's the first impression you leave on people, right alongside the way you talk, the way you act, and the way you keep your silence. I'm quite attentive and aesthetic about it, on myself and in how I read others.

Humility, no matter what you achieve, respect and kindness are the values I hold onto the most. Empathy and care matter to me: you should help people and bring them up with you rather than climb alone. I have little patience for self-centred people who take everything for granted and keep it all to themselves — your colleagues, your friends and your family are just as important as you are, and they deserve to be cared for.
